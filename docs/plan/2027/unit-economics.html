The market converged in mid-2026. GitHub (June 1), Cognition (June) and OpenAI (April) all moved off opaque units onto dollar-denominated, API-rate-pegged usage billing within three months of each other. The converged shape: $0 → $20 → $100 → $200 individual ladder, $19–40/seat team tier, custom enterprise, overage at raw API token rates. Sector margins forced it: Cursor ran negative gross margins through 2025, Replit sat below 10% (dipping negative) until its usage repricing, and inference eats ~23% of revenue at scaling AI SaaS — roughly 30 points below the classic SaaS baseline.

4Tenant vs. seat vs. usage — what each is for

Tenant
costs you ≈ $0

Not a cost-recovery lever — a packaging lever. Use an org base fee (Devin charges $80/mo minimum; Codegen $199 flat) to cover the fixed floor while tenant count is small and to gate team features (SSO, pooled credits, admin). Beyond ~10 tenants the floor is noise and the base fee is pure margin.

Seat
costs you <$1–7

Nearly pure margin, and the market will bear $19–40/seat for team plans ($100+ for premium seats à la Claude Team). Seats are your margin engine precisely where DMaker is differentiated: hybrid-runner (BYO-compute) teams cost you <$1/user — a $40 seat there is a ~97% margin, which no platform-billed competitor can match on their cloud offering.

Usage
costs you 92%+ of everything

Where the cost lives, so it must be metered and passed through with margin — never unlimited (flat-rate gets arbitraged 10× by heavy agents; every survivor now rate-limits or meters). Your plan's 10–20% credit markup matches sector norms (Cursor $0.25/M even on BYOK, OpenRouter 5.5%, Devin $2+/ACU). The caching spread — bill standard rates, pay 0.1× cache reads — is the hidden second margin.

The price ceiling is set by your suppliers. Your medium user costs you ~$92/mo — and Anthropic sells that same person Claude Max for $100. Platform-billed prices are anchored to ~$100–150 all-in for a medium user, so platform-billed margin is structurally thin at market anchors. The profit is in seats on BYO-compute, org minimums, credit markup, and the caching spread — not in reselling tokens at flat tiers.

Contribution per user × users, minus the fixed floor ($350/mo, stepping to $450 at 20+ users). Break-even is where each line crosses zero.

Flat-tier copycat = selling $20/$100 tiers with no usage markup or quota discipline — the strategy that put Cursor and Replit underwater. It stays below zero across this whole range.

Two readings of that table. First, break-even is small — a single-digit team — under any pricing that charges a real seat and marks up usage, because tenants and seats cost you almost nothing. Second, the copycat row shows the trap: the market's $20/$100/$200 ladder only works for the incumbents because their quotas quietly cap how much a tier can consume. If you sell flat tiers, the quota is the product's margin, not the price.

6What this implies for DMaker's pricing

  • Ship the converged ladder, keep the plan's mechanics. Free (local edition) → Pro ~$20–25 → Team $40/seat with an $80–100 org minimum → Enterprise custom. Familiar shape, no buyer education needed — but usage always flows through the credit wallet at cost +10–20%, never bundled "unlimited".
  • Lead with hybrid-runner seats. Your P1 revenue (seats, users bring compute) is also your best unit economics — ~$39 of $40 per seat is margin. Competitors can't follow without cannibalizing their platform-billed cloud.
  • Quota every flat tier. Refreshing windows (the Windsurf/Factory/Claude pattern) on anything not billed per-credit, so a heavy user hits overage instead of eating $280 of tokens on a $100 plan.
  • Price BYOK like Cursor prices it. ~$0.25/M or ~5% platform fee on user keys; the user costs you $7 and anchors your best conversion path from the free local edition.
  • Verify caching before promising margin. The whole cost model assumes cache-read-dominated sessions; without working cache passthrough the $92 medium user becomes $280–460 and every number above breaks. The plan's end-to-end cache verification gate is a commercial requirement, not a technical nicety.
